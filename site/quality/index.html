Quality & Inspection

Quality You Can Verify

Quality on this site is a process, not a badge. Here is how KVG builds accuracy into every mould and variable — from incoming material to final dimensional inspection — and how you can ask us to prove it.

Our philosophy

Quality Measured Against a Clear Vision

Everything in KVG's quality approach is anchored to one statement that has guided the company for years:

“Provide cost effective & highest quality Products in the shortest possible Lead time.”

— KVG Enterprises Vision

That single line sets three obligations that pull against each other in most workshops: keep the price competitive, hold the quality at the top of the range, and still ship quickly. KVG's answer is not to trade one off against the others but to design them out of conflict — by reading drawings accurately the first time, machining to repeatable tolerances on CNC equipment, and inspecting at the points where errors actually enter, so rework (the thing that quietly destroys both quality and lead time) is caught early rather than at the customer's line.

The practical test of quality for a glass plant is simple: does the part fit, does it run a full campaign, and does the next order match the last one. We hold ourselves to those outcomes, keep each part traceable to its drawing and part reference, and back it up with inspection records and references that we are glad to share on request.

What quality means here

  • ✓ The part fits your existing IS-machine holders and sections.
  • ✓ It holds up over a full production campaign.
  • ✓ The repeat order matches the first shipment, cavity for cavity.
  • ✓ Every part is traceable to its drawing and part reference.

Inspection process

Three Points Where We Catch Problems

Rather than a single check at the end, quality is verified at the three stages where deviation actually enters a part — the material, the machining, and the finished dimensions.

01

Incoming Material

Castings and raw material are checked on arrival against the specified grade — CI, SG iron, aluminium or Minox — before any machine time is committed. Catching a material issue here prevents it from being machined into an expensive finished reject.

02

In-Process Control

Critical dimensions are checked during machining, while the part is still on or near the machine and can be corrected. Working to the controlling drawing at each operation is what keeps a set of variables consistent with one another.

03

Final Dimensional Inspection

Before dispatch, the finished part is inspected against its drawing and part reference so the shipped item matches what was ordered — and so a future repeat order can be held to the same numbers.
